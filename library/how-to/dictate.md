# Dictate

*How-to guide · Resource Files library*

This guide is also the text alternative for the short video [dictate.mp4](../videos/dictate.mp4), which has no sound.

## The problem

Some learners know far more than they can type or spell. A blank page and a blinking cursor show the typing, not the knowledge.

## Why it matters for learners

- Learners with dyslexia, dysgraphia or motor difficulties.
- Learners who explain well out loud but freeze on paper.
- Anyone with a temporary injury.

Speaking is faster than typing for almost everyone.

## How to fix it

### In Word

**Home**, then **Dictate**. Wait for the microphone to show it is listening, then talk. Press it again to stop.

- Say **"full stop"**, **"comma"** and **"new line"** as you go.
- Set the language under the small arrow next to **Dictate**.

### Anywhere on Windows

Press **Windows key + H** in any text box: Teams, Forms, email, the browser.

## Teach it to every learner

Try one task where learners speak their answer first, then tidy it. Make it a normal way of working, not a special arrangement.

Check with your exams team before using it in assessment, so that it becomes a learner's normal way of working.

## Check it

Read back what was dictated. Names and technical words often need correcting.

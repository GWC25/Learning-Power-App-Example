# Colour contrast

*How-to guide · Resource Files library*

This guide is also the text alternative for the short video [colour-contrast.mp4](../videos/colour-contrast.mp4), which has no sound.

## The problem

Text that does not stand out from its background is hard or impossible to read. White on yellow, black on dark red and blue on red all fail. So does using colour alone to carry meaning, such as "red is assessed, green is practice".

## Why it matters for learners

- About one in twelve men and one in two hundred women are colour blind.
- A screen reader never says the colour.
- Projectors, bright sunlight, old screens and black and white printing all wash colour out.
- Everyone else reads low contrast text more slowly and gets tired sooner.

## How to fix it

1. **Go one light, one dark.** Contrast is light against dark, not colour against colour.
2. **Aim for at least 4.5 to 1** for normal text, and 3 to 1 for large text (WCAG 2.2, level AA).
3. **Keep the brand colour, flip the text.** White on a dark red passes comfortably.
4. **Never colour alone.** Add a word: "Unit 3 workbook: assessed". The colour can stay as a bonus.

## Check it

- **Review**, then **Check Accessibility**. Office flags hard-to-read text contrast.
- For anything else on screen, use the free **Colour Contrast Analyser** from TPGi. Use the eyedropper on the text, then on the background, and read the result. It can also show what colour blind learners see.
- Cannot install software? Use the WebAIM contrast checker in a browser.

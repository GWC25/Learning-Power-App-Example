# Captions and narration

*How-to guide · Resource Files library*

This guide is also the text alternative for the short video [captions-and-narration.mp4](../videos/captions-and-narration.mp4), which has no sound.

## The problem

A video with no captions says nothing when the sound is off, or too quiet, or drowned out by machines. A slide deck shared without narration leaves some learners with only the slides.

## Why it matters for learners

- Deaf and hard of hearing learners.
- Noisy workshops and shared spaces with no headphones.
- Learners reading in a second language, or meeting technical words for the first time.
- Anyone revising on a bus.

## How to fix it

### Presenting live in PowerPoint

**Slide Show**, then **Always Use Subtitles**. **Subtitle Settings** sets the spoken language, the subtitle language and where they appear.

### A video on a slide

Select the video, then **Insert Captions** (on the **Playback** tab in the desktop app, or **Video** in PowerPoint for the web). You need a caption file in the WebVTT format, ending .vtt.

To make one, upload the video to Microsoft Stream, which can generate captions. Watch them through and correct names and technical words, then download the caption file. Ask the digital team if you get stuck.

### Video from elsewhere

Look for **CC** on the player and switch it on before you show it.

### Narration

**Record**, then **From Beginning** records your voice over each slide. Narrated slides help learners who would rather listen. Turn subtitles on while you record so the narration is captioned too.

## Check it

Watch it with the sound off. Does it still make sense? Automatic captions are a starting point. Read them through and correct them.
